How San Diego Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? Its age tells you a lot. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows higher bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install when the first heat wave hits.

Minor issues rarely stay small for long. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
