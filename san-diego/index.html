Full-Service HVAC Care for San Diego Homes and Businesses

We service every type of heating and cooling equipment you'll find in local homes and businesses, from central air conditioners and gas furnaces to heat pumps and ductless mini-splits. A typical week for our residential technicians includes capacitor and contactor replacements, flame sensor cleanings, refrigerant leak searches, blower motor swaps, plus the occasional full system replacement or ductless addition. When Your System Fails in San Diego

Call us with a problem and a real person picks up, not a recording. Most calls get scheduled the same day or the next, and urgent calls, an AC that quits in a heat wave or a furnace that goes out on a bitter night, jump to the top of the schedule. We keep the process simple so you're not stuck waiting for a callback.

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that ignites and shuts off, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain overflowing into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Most of them come down to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and common fittings. You get the price before any work starts, with no surprises on the invoice.

A few minutes of caution while you wait for us can prevent a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us immediately.

How San Diego Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Many mid-season breakdowns start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and is also showing higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement in peak season.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected for a season, can take out the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off in your home. A quick call saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
